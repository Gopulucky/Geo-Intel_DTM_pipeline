Guide to Recommended Output Data Formats 
AI/ML Hackathon – Ministry of Panchayat Raj  
(Geospatial Intelligence Challenge) 
(Issued by Geo-Intel Lab, IIT Tirupati Navavishkar I-Hub Foundation, with inputs from OGC) 
1. Purpose of This Guide 
The Geospatial Intelligence Hackathon focuses on AI/ML-driven analysis of drone imagery and point-
cloud data to support governance-ready applications such as feature extraction, terrain modelling, and 
drainage network design. 
While participants are free to use any tools, platforms, or workflows, this guide provides recommended 
output data formats to ensure that hackathon results are: 
 Easy to review and validate 
 Interoperable across GIS and AI/ML environments 
 Reusable by government, academic, and startup ecosystems 
 Aligned with widely adopted, open geospatial practices 
This guidance does not change the problem statements, inputs, evaluation criteria, or expected 
outcomes of the hackathon. It only addresses how final outputs are packaged and shared. 
2. Recommended Output Formats 
Based on the nature of the problem statements and data involved, the following formats are recommended 
for final submissions. 
2.1 Raster Outputs — Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) 
Applies to: 
 Orthophotos 
 Digital Terrain Models (DTM) 
 Derived raster surfaces (slope, flow accumulation, masks, probability maps, etc.)

Recommended format: Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) 
Why COG: 
 Designed for efficient handling of large raster datasets 
 Supports fast access, visualization, and processing, especially for large raster datasets and cloud-
based workflows 
 Widely supported in QGIS, ArcGIS, GDAL, and AI/ML pipelines 
 Increasingly used in government and cloud-native geospatial systems 
Reference links: 
 COG Community Website (tools & ecosystem to generate COG format outputs): 
https://cogeo.org/ 
 OGC Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) Standard: 
https://www.ogc.org/standards/ogc-cloud-optimized-geotiff/ 
 
2.2 Vector Outputs — GeoPackage (GPKG) 
Applies to: 
 Building footprints (including roof-type attributes) 
 Roads 
 Drainage networks 
 Waterbodies 
 Asset locations (DTs, OHTs, wells, etc.) 
Recommended format: GeoPackage (GPKG) 
Why GeoPackage: 
 Single-file, portable container for vector GIS data 
 Supports multiple layers, attributes, and CRS in one file 
 Easier to manage and review than multi-file formats (e.g., shapefiles) 
 Fully supported in QGIS, ArcGIS, and common spatial databases 
Reference links:

2.3 Point-Cloud / LiDAR Outputs — LAS 
Applies to: 
 Drone-derived point-cloud data 
 Classified point clouds used for DTM generation 
 Intermediate or final 3D point-cloud outputs 
Recommended format: LAS 
Why LAS: 
 Widely adopted, open format for LiDAR and point-cloud exchange 
 Supported across GIS, surveying, and AI/ML ecosystems through widely used libraries and tools 
 Enables consistent sharing of classified and unclassified point data 
 Suitable for both research workflows and operational reuse 
Reference links: 
 LAS format overview: 
https://laspy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/intro.html 
 LAS Python library (laspy): 
https://laspy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/index.html 
 OGC LAS Standard: 
https://www.ogc.org/standards/las/

4. Important Clarifications for Participants 
 Participants may use any software, libraries, or platforms of their choice 
 This guide does not mandate tools, models, or workflows 
 No additional scoring is tied to format selection 
 The intent is to encourage clean, interoperable, and reusable outputs 
 These formats are already supported by most commonly used GIS and AI/ML tools 
5. Why This Matters 
By following this guidance, participants help ensure that hackathon outputs: 
 Align with real-world government and academic workflows 
 Can be easily validated, shared, and extended beyond the event 
 Introduce practical awareness of open, vendor-neutral geospatial formats 
 Contribute to a more interoperable and future-ready geospatial ecosystem 
This approach reflects IITNiF’s broader mission to foster scalable, standards-aware innovation while 
keeping the hackathon inclusive, practical, and outcome-focused.
