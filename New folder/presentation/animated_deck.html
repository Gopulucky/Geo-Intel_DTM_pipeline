The Problem

Every monsoon, villages flood — because no one can see how water actually moves through the terrain

Government drone surveys now produce rich 3D data as LAS/LAZ filesStandard formats storing millions of 3D laser-scan points. But these files are massive and highly technical. Planners can't use them directly — so the data sits unused while villages keep flooding.

Step 1 — Input
Point Cloud
Millions of 3D laser points from a drone flying over the village. Think of it as a dense cloud of dots representing every surface.
Step 2 — Need
DTM
Digital Terrain Model — a clean map of just the bare ground, with buildings and trees removed. Like peeling off everything to see the earth underneath.
Step 3 — Insight
Hydrology
Understanding where rainwater flows, pools, and creates problems — computed from terrain shape. The science of water movement.
Step 4 — Action
Drainage Design
Map-ready drainage lines that engineers can review and build on the ground. The actionable output.

Hydrology Analysis

Understanding how water behaves

Flow accumulation — traces where runoff naturally concentrates into streams. The more upstream area drains through a point, the higher its accumulation.
Stream extraction — pulls candidate drain alignments from flow data. These are lines where water already wants to go.
Catchments — splits the village into manageable drainage zones. Like dividing land into natural "buckets" that each drain to one point.
Flood vulnerability — ranks areas by how likely they are to waterlog. Combines sink depth, wetness index, and low elevation to score risk.
